\begin{exercise}{28}
Prove or disprove the following statement: For any two simplicial sets $X$ and
$Y$, the canonical map $h(X\times Y)\to hX\times hY$ is an isomorphism of categories.

\begin{proof}
The statement is true. Let $\Phi\colon h(X\times Y)\to hX\times hY$
be induced by the projections; it is the identity on objects.
For vertices $x\in X_0$ and $y\in Y_0$, the inclusions
$X\times\{y\}\to X\times Y$ and $\{x\}\times Y\to X\times Y$
induce functors
\[
 H_y\colon hX\longrightarrow h(X\times Y),
 \qquad V_x\colon hY\longrightarrow h(X\times Y)
\]
by Remark~1.2.6.

If $f\colon x\to x'$ and $g\colon y\to y'$ are edges,
the $2$-simplices $(s_1f,s_0g)$ and $(s_0f,s_1g)$ give,
by the simplicial identities of Exercise~\ref{ex:2} and
Definition~1.2.5,
\[
 V_{x'}([g])H_y([f])=[(f,g)]=H_{y'}([f])V_x([g]).
\]
Since edge classes generate the homotopy categories, commuting
one horizontal edge past one vertical edge at a time extends
this equality to all morphisms $\alpha\colon x\to x'$ in $hX$
and $\beta\colon y\to y'$ in $hY$:
\[
 V_{x'}(\beta)H_y(\alpha)=H_{y'}(\alpha)V_x(\beta).
\]
Define $\Psi\colon hX\times hY\to h(X\times Y)$ to be the
identity on objects and set
$\Psi(\alpha,\beta)=V_{x'}(\beta)H_y(\alpha)$.
This is well-defined because $H_y,V_{x'}$ are functors.
It preserves identities, and the displayed interchange law
gives
\[
 \Psi(\alpha',\beta')\Psi(\alpha,\beta)
 =\Psi(\alpha'\alpha,\beta'\beta)
\]
for composable pairs, so it is a functor.
The projections immediately give $\Phi\Psi=\mathrm{id}$.
The first displayed edge relation gives
$\Psi\Phi([(f,g)])=[(f,g)]$; since these edges generate
$h(X\times Y)$, also $\Psi\Phi=\mathrm{id}$.
Thus $\Phi$ is an isomorphism with inverse $\Psi$.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
