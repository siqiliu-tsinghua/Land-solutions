\begin{exercise}{96}
Let $\mathcal C$ be an ordinary category. Consider a pushout of categories
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
 \displaystyle\coprod_{f\in\mathrm{Mor}(\mathcal C)}[1]
   \arrow[r] \arrow[d]
   & \mathcal C \arrow[d] \\
 \displaystyle\coprod_{f\in\mathrm{Mor}(\mathcal C)}J
   \arrow[r] & \mathcal D
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where $\mathrm{Mor}(\mathcal C)$ denotes the set of all morphisms of
$\mathcal C$. Show that $\mathcal D$ is a groupoid.

\begin{proof}
Here $J$ is the ordinary walking isomorphism.
Write $j\colon\mathcal C\to\mathcal D$ and
$b\colon\coprod_f J\to\mathcal D$ for the canonical functors.
For each $f$ of $\mathcal C$, the $f$-th copy of $J$ supplies an inverse
to $j(f)$, since its forward isomorphism maps to $j(f)$.
Every morphism in the image of $b$ is also invertible, with its inverse
in the same image.

Let $\mathcal D'$ be the wide subcategory of $\mathcal D$ generated by
these two images. Its generators have inverses among the generators,
so $\mathcal D'$ is a groupoid.
Both canonical functors factor through $\mathcal D'$.
The pushout property gives $r\colon\mathcal D\to\mathcal D'$;
if $\iota\colon\mathcal D'\hookrightarrow\mathcal D$ is the inclusion,
uniqueness in that property gives $\iota r=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal D}$.
Every morphism of $\mathcal D$ therefore belongs to $\mathcal D'$,
so $\mathcal D=\mathcal D'$ is a groupoid.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
