\begin{exercise}{101}
Let $X\to X'$ and $Y\to Y'$ be Joyal equivalences between simplicial sets.
Show that both maps $X\amalg Y\to X'\amalg Y'$ and
$X\times Y\to X'\times Y'$ are Joyal equivalences.

\begin{proof}
Write $f\colon X\to X'$ and $g\colon Y\to Y'$ for the given maps.
For any simplicial set $K$ and $\infty$-category $\mathcal E$, the
exponential law identifies restriction along $f\times\mathrm{id}_K$ with
\[
 \mathrm{Fun}\bigl(X',\mathrm{Fun}(K,\mathcal E)\bigr)
 \xrightarrow{f^*}
 \mathrm{Fun}\bigl(X,\mathrm{Fun}(K,\mathcal E)\bigr).
\]
Since $\mathrm{Fun}(K,\mathcal E)$ is an $\infty$-category by
Corollary~1.3.38, this restriction is a Joyal equivalence by
Definition~2.2.9. Thus $f\times\mathrm{id}_K$ is a Joyal equivalence.
By symmetry the same holds for $\mathrm{id}_K\times g$.
Factoring $f\times g$ into these two types of maps, and testing the
composite, proves the product assertion.

For the coproduct, the natural isomorphisms
\[
 \mathrm{Fun}(X\amalg Y,\mathcal E)
 \cong\mathrm{Fun}(X,\mathcal E)\times\mathrm{Fun}(Y,\mathcal E)
\]
and their primed versions identify restriction along $f\amalg g$ with
$f^*\times g^*$. Both factors are Joyal equivalences, so the product
assertion and Observation~2.2.10 show that this restriction is a Joyal
equivalence. Definition~2.2.9 gives the coproduct assertion.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
