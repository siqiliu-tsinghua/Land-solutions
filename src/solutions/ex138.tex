\begin{exercise}{138}
Show that there exists $\mathcal C$ and $K$ such that $\mathrm{Fun}(K,\mathcal C)$ has a terminal object but
$\mathcal C$ does not.

\begin{proof}
Take $K=\varnothing$ and $\mathcal C=\Delta^0\amalg\Delta^0$, the nerve
of the discrete category on two objects.
Since $\Delta^n\times K=\varnothing$ for every $n$,
$\mathrm{Fun}(K,\mathcal C)\cong\Delta^0$, whose unique object is terminal.
Neither object of $\mathcal C$ is terminal: its mapping space from
the other object is empty.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
