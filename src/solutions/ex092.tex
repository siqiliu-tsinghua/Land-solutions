\begin{exercise}{92}
Let $\mathcal C$ be an $\infty$-category and $S$ a set of morphisms of $\mathcal C$.
Then $S$ is called \emph{saturated} if it coincides with the set $\bar{S}$ of all
morphisms that are sent to equivalences under the functor
$\mathcal C\to\mathcal C[S^{-1}]$. Show that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item If $S\subseteq T$ and $T$ is saturated, then $\bar{S}\subseteq T$ as well.
\item Two sets of morphisms $S$ and $T$ of $\mathcal C$ give rise to Joyal-equivalent
localizations (compatible with the map from $\mathcal C$) if and only if
$\bar{S}=\bar{T}$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proof}
Write $\gamma_U\colon\mathcal C\to\mathcal C[U^{-1}]$ for localization along
$U$; in particular $U\subseteq\bar{U}$ by Definition~2.4.2.
Naturally equivalent functors invert exactly the same morphisms:
the proof of Exercise~\ref{ex:89} gives a natural isomorphism between their
induced homotopy-category functors, so the images of each morphism are
conjugate by isomorphisms (Definition~1.2.22).

If $F\colon\mathcal C\to\mathcal E$ inverts $S$, Definition~2.4.2 gives
$F\simeq F'\gamma_S$ for some $F'\colon\mathcal C[S^{-1}]\to\mathcal E$.
Since $F'$ preserves equivalences, the preceding observation shows that
$F$ inverts $\bar{S}$.
As $S\subseteq\bar{S}$, we obtain
$\mathrm{Fun}^S(\mathcal C,\mathcal E)
=\mathrm{Fun}^{\bar{S}}(\mathcal C,\mathcal E)$
for every $\mathcal E$.
Thus $\gamma_S$ is also a localization along $\bar{S}$.

(1) Since $\gamma_T$ inverts $S\subseteq T$, it inverts $\bar{S}$ by the
preceding paragraph. Hence $\bar{S}\subseteq\bar{T}=T$.

(2) If $\bar{S}=\bar{T}$, both localization functors localize along this
common set. Lemma~2.4.4 gives a Joyal equivalence $F$ between their targets
with $F\gamma_S\simeq\gamma_T$.
Conversely, suppose such an $F$ exists.
By Exercise~\ref{ex:89} and Definition~1.2.22, a Joyal equivalence preserves
and reflects equivalences.
Therefore $\gamma_S$ and $F\gamma_S$ invert the same morphisms;
so do $F\gamma_S$ and $\gamma_T$ by their natural equivalence.
Their inverted sets are precisely $\bar{S}$ and $\bar{T}$, proving equality.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
