\begin{exercise}{57}
Show that a saturated set $S$ in a category $\mathcal C$ contains all isomorphisms. Find an
example of a category $\mathcal C$ and a semi-saturated set $S$ of morphisms in $\mathcal C$ which is non-empty
and does not contain all isomorphisms.

\begin{proof}
Closure under the empty coproduct gives
$\mathrm{id}_{\varnothing}\in S$.
Exercise~\ref{ex:56}(1) then puts every isomorphism in $S$.

For the example, take $\mathcal C=\mathrm{Set}$ and let $T$
consist of the injective maps $A\to B$ with $B\ne\varnothing$.
A pushout of an injection attaches its complement to the new
source, so is injective; its target receives a map from the
original nonempty target and is therefore nonempty.
A retract of an injection is injective: the retract's domain
section, followed by the injection, detects equality.
Its target is also nonempty, since it receives the retraction
map from the original nonempty target.

For a sequence of maps in $T$, the structure maps to its colimit
are injective: equality there is detected at a common finite stage,
by the filtered-set calculation in Exercise~\ref{ex:55},
and every intervening map is injective.
The colimit is nonempty because it receives a map from the
nonempty object at stage $1$.
Thus $T$ is semi-saturated and nonempty
($\varnothing\to\{*\}\in T$), but excludes the isomorphism
$\mathrm{id}_{\varnothing}$.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
