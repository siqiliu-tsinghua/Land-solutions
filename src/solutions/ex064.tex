\begin{exercise}{64}
Show that if $f\colon y\to z$ is an equivalence, then
\[
 \mathrm{map}_{\mathcal C}(x,y)
 \simeq \mathrm{map}_{\mathcal C}(x,y)\times\Delta^0
 \xrightarrow{f}
 \mathrm{map}_{\mathcal C}(x,y)\times\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal C}(y,z)
 \longrightarrow \mathrm{map}_{\mathcal C}(x,z)
\]
is a homotopy equivalence.

\begin{proof}
Write $M(u,v)=\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal C}(u,v)$ and
$\mu(a,b)$ for the chosen composition, with $b$ after $a$.
The spaces $M(u,v)$ are Kan complexes
(Proposition~1.3.48 and Corollary~2.1.12).
We first record the homotopies needed for the inverse.

If $K\hookrightarrow L$ is inner-anodyne, then
$\mathcal C^L\to\mathcal C^K$ is a trivial fibration
(Theorem~1.3.37).
For a family $Q\times K\to\mathcal C$, pull this map back
to a trivial fibration over $Q$.
Its sections exist and form a contractible Kan complex
(Lemma~1.3.46).
Transposing a path between sections shows that any two
extensions $Q\times L\to\mathcal C$ are homotopic relative
to $Q\times K$.
In particular, for $K=I^2\subseteq\Delta^2=L$, restriction
to the long edge compares any two choices of composition
for the same family of composable edges.
Comparing with the degenerate triangle families gives the
unit homotopies
\[
 \mu(\mathrm{id}_u,a)\simeq a,
 \qquad \mu(a,\mathrm{id}_v)\simeq a
 \quad(a\in M(u,v)).
\]

For the associativity comparison, put
$Q=M(w,x)\times M(x,y)\times M(y,z)$ and set
\[
 L=\Delta^{\{0,1,2\}}\cup\Delta^{\{0,2,3\}},
 \qquad
 L'=\Delta^{\{1,2,3\}}\cup\Delta^{\{0,1,3\}}.
\]
Both inclusions into $\Delta^3$ are inner-anodyne:
to $L$ attach the face $123$ along its inner $2$-horn,
obtaining $\Lambda^3_2$, and then attach $\Delta^3$;
for $L'$ attach $012$ along its inner $2$-horn,
obtaining $\Lambda^3_1$, and then attach $\Delta^3$.
For the universal triple $(a,b,c)$ over $Q$, the chosen
composition triangles for $(a,b)$ and $(\mu(a,b),c)$
glue on the common edge $02$ to a family on $L$.
Similarly, those for $(b,c)$ and $(a,\mu(b,c))$ glue on
$L'$ along $13$.
Extend both families over $\Delta^3$ by the preceding
comparison principle.
They have the same restriction to $Q\times I^3$.
Since $I^3\hookrightarrow\Delta^3$ is inner-anodyne
(Remark~1.3.45), they are homotopic relative to this spine.
Restricting to $03$ gives a homotopy
\[
 \mu(\mu(a,b),c)\simeq\mu(a,\mu(b,c))
\]
of maps $Q\to M(w,z)$: the endpoints $w,z$ stay fixed
throughout because the homotopy fixes the spine.

By Lemma~1.2.23 there are edges
$g_L,g_R\colon z\to y$ and triangles with spines
$(f,g_L)$, $(g_R,f)$ and long edges
$\mathrm{id}_y$, $\mathrm{id}_z$, respectively.
The $I^2$ comparison above therefore gives paths
\[
 \mu(f,g_L)\simeq\mathrm{id}_y,
 \qquad \mu(g_R,f)\simeq\mathrm{id}_z.
\]
Let $u\colon M(x,y)\to M(x,z)$ be postcomposition with
$f$, and let $a,b\colon M(x,z)\to M(x,y)$ be
postcomposition with $g_L,g_R$, respectively.
Associativity, these paths, and the unit homotopies give
$au\simeq\mathrm{id}$ and $ub\simeq\mathrm{id}$.
In $\pi(\mathrm{Kan})$ it follows that
\[
 [a]=[a][u][b]=[b].
\]
Thus $[a]$ is a two-sided inverse of $[u]$, which says
that $a$ is a homotopy inverse of the displayed map.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
