\chapter{Errata to the Text}
\label{app:text-errata}
Page numbers below refer to the printed pages of Land's book.
Tentative readings are identified as such.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~4, Definition 1.1.1(3).}\enspace
The universe is printed as $\mathcal V_{\kappa_2}$. The translator suggests $\mathcal V_{<\kappa_2}$, consistently with the surrounding universe notation; the missing inequality is tentative.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~9, Observation 1.1.15, continuation.}\enspace
The last two assertions interchange cones and cocones. With the conventions just defined, a colimit is an initial object of $\mathrm{Fun}_F(I^{\rhd},\mathcal C)$, the category of cocones, and a limit is a terminal object of $\mathrm{Fun}_F(I^{\lhd},\mathcal C)$, the category of cones.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~10, Definition 1.1.20.}\enspace
The parenthesis contains the German word \emph{statt} in an English sentence. The translator flags this as an apparent editorial oversight; \emph{statt} means ``instead of.''

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~11, Lemma 1.1.22, proof.}\enspace
Replace $x\in\mathcal E$ by $x\in I$. The category $\mathcal E$ is undefined, and $x$ indexes the components of a natural transformation between functors with domain $I$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~13, Fact 1.1.27, second formula.}\enspace
Replace $\lim_{c\in(\mathcal C_0)_{x/}}F(x)$ by $\lim_{c\in(\mathcal C_0)_{y/}}F(c)$. The right Kan extension is evaluated at $y$, and the diagram is evaluated at its indexing object $c$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~18, Lemma 1.1.40, proof (2).}\enspace
The horn coequalizer uses an undefined index $k$ and ends in $\Lambda_i^n$. The translator flags the inconsistent indices and suggests $\Lambda_j^n$ for the final term. This note records the indexing problem, rather than prescribing a fully corrected coequalizer.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~23, Lemma 1.1.53, proof.}\enspace
Apparent correction: Replace $\mathrm{sk}_2(\Delta^2)$ by $\mathrm{sk}_2(\Delta^n)$. The asserted isomorphism compares the $2$-skeleta of an $n$-horn and its ambient $n$-simplex for $n\ge4$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~24, Section 1.2, introductory paragraph.}\enspace
The phrase ``import tool'' is presumably ``important tool.''

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~27, Lemma 1.2.9, proof of (3), diagram.}\enspace
Apparent correction: The edge with both endpoints $z$ is labelled $\mathrm{id}_y$. Its label should be $\mathrm{id}_z$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~28, Lemma 1.2.10, proof, diagram.}\enspace
The edge $h$ has both endpoints labelled $z$. For general composable $f,g,h$, write $h\colon z\to w$ and label the final vertex $w$; the argument should not restrict $h$ to an endomorphism.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~32, Corollary 1.2.20.}\enspace
Read ``Theorem 1.1.52'' in place of ``Proposition 1.1.52,'' in accordance with the heading of the cited result.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~38, Definition 1.2.34, right-hand unit diagram.}\enspace
The diagonal arrow in the right-hand diagram (involving $\eta_l$) is unlabelled. It apparently should be labelled $\mathrm{id}$, as in the other unit diagram.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~43, Corollary 1.2.45, proof.}\enspace
Apparent correction: Replace ``in constant'' by ``is constant.''

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~45, Lemma 1.2.50, final sentence of proof.}\enspace
Apparent correction: In the phrase ``a square which both vertical maps being isomorphisms,'' replace ``which'' by ``with.'' The intended assertion is that inverting both vertical isomorphisms preserves commutativity.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~52, Lemma 1.2.69, proof.}\enspace
Apparent correction: In the description of the sub-poset $P^{[n]\setminus\{i\}}_{0,n}\subseteq P^{[n]}_{0,n}$, replace $I\in P^{[n]}_{k,l}$ by $I\in P^{[n]}_{0,n}$, matching the endpoints under discussion.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~59, Remark 1.3.6, countable compositions.}\enspace
The two occurrences of membership in $S$ should be membership in $T$, the saturated set under discussion. Also read ``is contained in'' for ``is contained is.''

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~60, Lemma 1.3.8, proof.}\enspace
Replace $\operatorname{colim}_i X_i$ by $\operatorname{colim}_i A_i$: the sequential diagram is denoted $A$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~74, Lemma 1.3.34, proof, case $i=0$.}\enspace
Replace $\Delta^m\cup\Lambda^2_1$ by $\Delta^m\times\Lambda^2_1$. The face under discussion lies in the product used throughout the construction.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~80, Lemma 1.3.46, first sentence of proof.}\enspace
The notation $\mathrm{Hom}(A,X)$ should denote the internal hom $\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(A,X)$, and likewise for $Y$. The assertion concerns a trivial fibration of simplicial sets, not a map of hom-sets.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~91, Lemma 1.4.20, first sentence of proof.}\enspace
Replace $X_{/p}$ by $X_{p/}$, consistently with the slice construction used in the statement and the remainder of the proof.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~100, Remark 2.1.9, proof and continuation on p.101.}\enspace
Six occurrences of $f$ should be $\varphi$: the left-inverse assertion, the spine $(f,\alpha,f)$, $s_1(f)$, $s_0(f)$, the right-inverse assertion, and the conclusion that $f$ is an equivalence. The edge introduced in the statement is $\varphi$; no $f$ is defined.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~101, Corollary 2.1.10, last lifting diagram.}\enspace
Replace $\Lambda^n_0$ and $\Delta^n$ by $\Lambda^2_0$ and $\Delta^2$. The prescribed edges $01$ and $02$ in the preceding construction form a $2$-horn.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~106, Corollary 2.2.2, reformulation.}\enspace
Replace $x\in K$ by $x\in K_0$. The condition tests the components of the natural transformation at vertices.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~113, Proposition 2.2.12, end of proof.}\enspace
Replace ``for any $\infty$-category $Y$'' by ``for any $\infty$-category $\mathcal C$.'' Here $Y$ already denotes the target simplicial set of $f$, whereas $\mathcal C$ is the arbitrary target in the functor categories.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~116, Lemma 2.2.17, displayed core map in proof.}\enspace
In $(\mathcal C^K\times_{\mathcal D^K}\mathcal C^L)^{\simeq}$, replace the final factor $\mathcal C^L$ by $\mathcal D^L$. This is the target of the pullback-power map induced by $p$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~117, Remark 2.2.19, first displayed map.}\enspace
As in Lemma 2.2.17, the last factor $\mathcal C^L$ in the pullback must be $\mathcal D^L$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~123, Corollary 2.3.12, proof, based path-space square.}\enspace
The right vertical map is labelled $s$ in the diagram and $t$ in the prose. Both refer there to evaluation at $0$; the labels should be made consistent. This records the translator's observation without imposing a different endpoint convention.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~129, Lemma 2.3.19, first lifting diagram in proof.}\enspace
Replace the vertical label $p$ by $p^{\prime}$. The lift $\beta$ is taken through the fibration $p^{\prime}$, as the following equality $p^{\prime}\beta=p\alpha$ confirms.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~129, Lemma 2.3.19, surjectivity argument.}\enspace
The map restricting to an equivalence between the fibres is $f$, not $f^{\prime}$. At this stage $f^{\prime}$ has already been reduced to the identity on the base.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~129, Lemma 2.3.19, surjectivity argument.}\enspace
The chosen point of $F^{\prime}_x$ corresponding to $[y]$ should be named $y^{\prime}$, not $y$, consistently with the following sentence.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~130, Theorem 2.3.20, proof.}\enspace
In the two sentences about a path from $\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal C}$ and the resulting natural equivalence, replace $fg$ by $gf$. The composite $gf$ is an endofunctor of $\mathcal C$; $fg$ is an endofunctor of $\mathcal D$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~136, Lemma 2.4.6, first paragraph of proof.}\enspace
The first sentence writes $f\colon\mathcal C\to X$, but the pushout diagram labels $\mathcal C\to Y$ by $f$. Use $f\colon\mathcal C\to Y$ consistently; the anodyne map to $X$ is the composite $gf$, where $g\colon Y\to X$ is introduced next.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~137, Lemma 2.4.6, proof.}\enspace
Replace the reason ``since $Y\to X$ is an inner fibration'' by the facts that $g\colon Y\to X$ is inner-anodyne and $\mathcal D\to *$ is an inner fibration. These are the hypotheses needed in Theorem 1.3.37(2) for restriction into $\mathcal D$ to be a trivial fibration.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\begingroup\raggedright
\noindent\textbf{p.~137, Lemma 2.4.6, proof, bottom row of diagram.}\enspace
The targets in $\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal C_1}\mathrm{Fun}(J,\mathcal C)$ and $\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal C_1}\mathrm{Fun}^{\simeq}(\Delta^1,\mathcal C)$ should both be $\mathcal D$. The square is obtained by mapping the defining pushout of $Y$ into $\mathcal D$.
\par\endgroup

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~140, Lemma 2.4.9, proof.}\enspace
The two references to the composite $Fi$ should be to $iF\colon\mathrm{sSet}\to\mathrm{sSet}$, with fibrant replacement $F$ followed by the inclusion $i$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~149, Proposition 2.5.13, proof.}\enspace
Replace ``the 3-for-2 property for Joyal fibrations'' by ``the 3-for-2 property for Joyal equivalences.'' It is the equivalence property of $B^{\prime}\to B$ that is inferred here.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~164, Lemma 3.1.2.}\enspace
Replace ``a morphism in $Y$'' by ``a morphism in $X$.'' The edge $f\colon x\to y$ is the edge whose $p$-cartesianness is being tested.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~169, Corollary 3.1.13, diagram.}\enspace
Replace the upper-right mapping space $\mathrm{map}^{?}_{\mathcal E}(x,y)$ by $\mathrm{map}^{?}_{\mathcal C}(x,y)$, and the lower-right $\mathrm{map}^{?}_{\mathcal C}(p(x),p(y))$ by $\mathrm{map}^{?}_{\mathcal D}(p(x),p(y))$. The isofibration in this corollary is $\mathcal C\to\mathcal D$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~172, Remark 3.1.18, displayed map.}\enspace
The printed map mixes the ordinary source slice $\mathcal E_{/f}$ with fat slices and has base $\mathcal C^{/(y)}$. Following Remark 3.1.3, read $\mathcal E^{/f}\to\mathcal E^{/y}\times_{\mathcal C_{/p(y)}}\mathcal C^{/pf}$. This both restores $p(y)$ and uses the matching fat source slice.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~173, Definition 3.1.20, informal reformulation.}\enspace
Replace ``any morphism in $\mathcal C$'' by ``any morphism in $Y$.'' The base of the fibration in the definition is $Y$; $\mathcal C$ is undefined.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~174, Proposition 3.1.23, two diagrams in proof.}\enspace
The vertex $1$ is labelled $p(y)$ in the base diagram and $y$ in the lifted diagram. It should be $p(x^{\prime})=p(x)$ and $x^{\prime}$, respectively, so that the edge labelled $\mathrm{id}$ and the fibre edge $\psi$ have the stated endpoints.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~176, Theorem 3.1.27, proof.}\enspace
Before choosing the cartesian lift, introduce an arbitrary edge $\alpha\colon x\to y$ of $\mathcal E$. The edge $\hat\alpha\colon x^{\prime}\to y$ is a $p$-cartesian lift of $p(\alpha)$, rather than a lift of an undefined base edge $\alpha$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~181, Proposition 3.2.9, proof, pullback construction.}\enspace
Two occurrences of $X\times_Y\Delta^2$ should be $X\times_S\Delta^2$. The base map in the pullback is $p\tau\colon\Delta^2\to S$; no $Y$ has been introduced.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~182, Proposition 3.2.9, proof, diagram.}\enspace
Restore the missing product sign in the pullback object $J^{\sharp}\times_S X$. The prose already specifies this pullback.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~187, Lemma 3.2.17, first paragraph of proof.}\enspace
Replace $(\Lambda^n_0)^{\sharp}$ by $(\Lambda^n_i)^{\sharp}$ in the assertion quantified over $0\le i<n$. The inner horns are handled next, before the separate case $i=0$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~196, Lemma 3.3.6, proof, displayed sequence.}\enspace
Replace the third arrow label $\alpha_0$ by $\alpha_3$, consistently with the indexing of the composable sequence.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~202, Proposition 3.3.18, first sentence of proof.}\enspace
The target of $g_*\colon\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal C}(x,y)\to\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal C}(y,z)$ should be $\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal C}(x,z)$, as in the statement and the postcomposition construction.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~203, Proposition 3.3.18, last displayed formula in proof.}\enspace
Delete the extra multiplication sign in $\mathcal C_{/g}\times_{\mathcal C_{/z}}\times\{f\}$. The intended pullback is $\mathcal C_{/g}\times_{\mathcal C_{/z}}\{f\}$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~203, Corollary 3.3.19, last sentence of proof.}\enspace
Replace the hypothesis that $f$ is fully faithful by the hypothesis that $\mathcal C\hookrightarrow\mathcal D$ is fully faithful. Here $f$ is an object of $\mathcal C_{/z}$, not the functor in that hypothesis.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~209, Proposition 4.1.5, proof.}\enspace
Replace $b\colon[k]\to[n]$ by $\beta_i\colon[k]\to[n]$ in the surjectivity assertion, consistently with the simplex $(\alpha_i,\beta_i)$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~216, Proposition 4.2.10, proof.}\enspace
In the sentence asserting that $p_*$ is a right fibration because $p$ is, read ``left'' in both places. The map $p$ was chosen to be a left fibration, and Theorem 1.3.37 preserves this handedness for the induced functor map.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~223, Theorem 4.3.11, first sentence of proof.}\enspace
Apparently, ``A cocone'' should read ``A cone,'' in the terminology of the preceding statement.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~231, Proposition 4.3.26.}\enspace
The colimit of $F|_C$ is denoted $y$ a second time. Replace that occurrence by $z$, matching the pushout $y\amalg_x z$ in the conclusion and proof.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~232, Proposition 4.3.27, proof.}\enspace
Replace $\mathcal C^{G_i}$ by the fat slice $\mathcal C^{G_i/}$ in the equivalence with $\mathcal C^{G(i)/}$. The slash is missing.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~234, Definition 4.3.30, last sentence.}\enspace
The two assertions that $F$ preserves colimits should have subject $f$. The functor between the $\infty$-categories is $f\colon\mathcal C\to\mathcal D$; $F$ denotes the diagram to which it is applied.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~236, Theorem 4.3.37, proof of essential surjectivity.}\enspace
Replace ``an object of $\mathcal C^{/F}$'' by ``an object of $\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{/F}$.'' The diagram $F$ has values in $\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~237, Definition 4.4.2, both clauses.}\enspace
The fibrations are printed as $p\colon X\to Y$. Replace the target $Y$ by $L$ in both the cofinal and coinitial clauses, as required by $\mathrm{Fun}_L(L,X)$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~242, Corollary 4.4.10.}\enspace
The two occurrences of $f$ as the subject of ``admits'' or ``preserves this colimit'' should be $p$, according to the translator's correction. The diagram is $p\colon L\to\mathcal C$; the map $f\colon K\to L$ is its change of indexing shape.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~242, Lemma 4.4.11, proof.}\enspace
The object $\varphi$ should lie in $\mathrm{Fun}_L(L,K)$, not $\mathrm{Fun}_L(L,X)$. Its image is compared with $\mathrm{id}_K$ and gives a section $L\to K$.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~247, Before Corollary 4.4.22.}\enspace
The corollary is introduced as the actual statement proved by Quillen. As the translator notes, Quillen's original hypothesis concerns $\mathcal C_{/d}$, whose objects are pairs $(c,F(c)\to d)$; the displayed hypothesis on $\mathcal C_{d/}$ is its dual. The displayed corollary remains valid, but should be identified as the dual form of the original statement.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~262, Proposition 5.1.27, end of proof.}\enspace
Replace ``right adjoint'' by ``left adjoint'' in the last appeal to Proposition 5.1.16. Postcomposition with $\operatorname{colim}_K$ is the left adjoint of postcomposition with the constant functor.

\par\addvspace{.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{p.~267, Corollary 5.2.5, end of proof.}\enspace
Replace the reference to Proposition 5.1.24 by Remark 5.1.25. The claim concerns the right adjoint producing limits, whereas Proposition 5.1.24 addresses colimits and a left adjoint.
