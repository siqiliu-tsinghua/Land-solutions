\begin{exercise}{113}
Let $\mathcal C$ be an $\infty$-category and consider for each $i\ge0$ a morphism
$x_i\to x_{i+1}$ between objects in $\mathcal C$. Show that these maps assemble into a functor
$\mathbb N\to\mathcal C$.

\begin{proof}
For $n\ge0$, the inclusion $\{n\}\hookrightarrow\Delta^n$ is right-anodyne:
adjoin the preceding vertices one at a time, using the face inclusions
$\Delta^{\{1,\ldots,m\}}\hookrightarrow\Delta^m$, which are right-anodyne
by Lemma~1.3.21(3).
Lemma~1.4.22, applied to $\{n\}\hookrightarrow\Delta^n$ and
$\varnothing\hookrightarrow\Delta^0$, therefore makes
\[
 \Delta^n\cup_{\{n\}}\Delta^{\{n,n+1\}}
 \hookrightarrow\Delta^{n+1}
\]
inner-anodyne.

Start with the vertex $x_0\colon\Delta^0\to\mathcal C$.
Given $F_n\colon\Delta^n\to\mathcal C$ realizing the first $n$ prescribed
edges, glue it to $x_n\to x_{n+1}$ on the displayed domain.
The inner-anodyne inclusion extends this to $F_{n+1}$, with
$F_{n+1}|_{\Delta^n}=F_n$ and the new last edge exactly as prescribed.
Thus the $F_n$ are compatible.
Since every finite chain of natural numbers lies in some $[n]$,
$N(\mathbb N)=\operatorname{colim}_n\Delta^n$.
The compatible maps induce the desired functor, retaining every
specified object and edge.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
