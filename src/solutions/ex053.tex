\begin{exercise}{53}
A category $I$ is called \emph{filtered} if every functor $K\to I$ from a finite category $K$
extends over the inclusion $K\to K^{\rhd}$. Show that a poset (viewed as a category) is filtered
if and only if
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item for every finite collection of objects $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ of $I$, there exists an object $X$ of $I$
equipped with maps $X_k\to X$ for all $k=1,\ldots,n$;
\item any two morphisms $f,g\colon X\to Y$ can be equalized, i.e., there exists a morphism $h\colon Y\to
Z$ such that $hf=hg$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proof}
In fact, (1) and (2) characterize filteredness for any category $I$.
The finite collection in (1) may be empty, so (1) includes
nonemptiness of $I$.
If $I$ is filtered, applying the definition to finite discrete
categories gives (1).
Apply it also to the category consisting of a parallel pair
$f,g\colon X\rightrightarrows Y$.
Writing the resulting cone legs as $k\colon X\to Z$ and
$h\colon Y\to Z$ gives $hf=k=hg$, proving (2).

Conversely, assume (1) and (2), and let $F\colon K\to I$
be a functor from a finite category.
By (1), choose maps $\delta_k\colon F(k)\to T$ for all objects $k$.
Starting with $u=\mathrm{id}_T$, process the finitely many
morphisms $a\colon k\to l$ of $K$ in turn.
Apply (2) to the parallel maps
$u\delta_lF(a),u\delta_k$ and postcompose $u$ with the
resulting equalizing map.
Previously established equalities persist under postcomposition,
so the final map $u\colon T\to U$ satisfies
\[
 u\delta_lF(a)=u\delta_k
 \qquad\text{for every }a\colon k\to l\text{ in }K.
\]
Thus the legs $u\delta_k$ define an extension
$K^{\rhd}\to I$ with new object $U$.
This includes empty $K$, since (1) still supplies $T$.

For a poset, (2) is automatic: parallel maps are equal,
so one can take $h=\mathrm{id}_Y$.
The criterion therefore applies, and (1) says exactly that
every finite collection has an upper bound.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
