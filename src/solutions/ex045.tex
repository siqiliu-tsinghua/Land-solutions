\begin{exercise}{45}
Show that there exist monomorphisms $X\to Y$ where both $X$ and $Y$ are $\infty$-categories,
but $X$ is not a sub-$\infty$-category in the sense of Definition~1.2.76.

\begin{proof}
Take the degreewise injective map
\[
 X=\mathcal S(\{0\})\cong\Delta^0
 \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow Y=\mathcal S([0,1]).
\]
Both are Kan complexes by Lemma~1.1.47, hence
$\infty$-categories. The image of $X$ consists exactly of
the constant singular simplices at $0$.
Consider the singular $2$-simplex
\[
 \sigma\colon\Delta^2_{\mathrm{top}}\longrightarrow[0,1],
 \qquad \sigma(t_0,t_1,t_2)=27t_0t_1t_2.
\]
It takes values in $[0,1]$ by the arithmetic--geometric
mean inequality. Every boundary edge is constant at $0$,
so its spine lies in $X$, but
$\sigma(1/3,1/3,1/3)=1$ shows that $\sigma\notin X_2$.
If $X$ were a sub-$\infty$-category, its determining edge set
would be $X_1$, and Definition~1.2.76 would require every
simplex of $Y$ with spine in $X_1$ to belong to $X$.
The simplex $\sigma$ contradicts this requirement.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
