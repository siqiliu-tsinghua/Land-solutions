\begin{exercise}{115}
Let $\mathcal C$ be an $\infty$-category and consider the inner fibration $p\colon\mathcal C\to\Delta^0$. Show
that a morphism $f$ in $\mathcal C$ is $p$-(co)cartesian if and only if $f$ is an equivalence.

\begin{proof}
If $f$ is an equivalence, Theorem~2.1.8 and its opposite form
(Remark~2.1.11) solve exactly the outer-horn problems in Definition~3.1.1.
Thus $f$ is both $p$-cartesian and $p$-cocartesian.

Conversely, suppose $f\colon x\to y$ is $p$-cartesian.
Corollary~3.1.16, interpreted as in Remark~3.1.17, gives a homotopy equivalence
\[
 f_*\colon\operatorname{map}_{\mathcal C}(z,x)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{map}_{\mathcal C}(z,y)
\]
for every $z$, since both mapping spaces in the base are points.
On connected components, these are bijections
$[f]_*\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{h\mathcal C}(z,x)
\to\operatorname{Hom}_{h\mathcal C}(z,y)$.
Surjectivity for $z=y$ gives $g\colon y\to x$ in $h\mathcal C$ with
$[f]g=\mathrm{id}_y$.
Since $[f](g[f])=[f]=[f]\mathrm{id}_x$, injectivity for $z=x$ gives
$g[f]=\mathrm{id}_x$.
Hence $[f]$ is an isomorphism and $f$ is an equivalence.
Taking opposites proves the cocartesian converse as well:
Definition~3.1.1 interchanges the two horn conditions, and
Exercise~\ref{ex:48} preserves $\infty$-categories and equivalence edges.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
