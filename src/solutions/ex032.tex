\begin{exercise}{32}
Let $X$ be a composer with the extension property for inner $3$-horns. Show
that for any two composable morphisms $f\colon x\to y$ and $g\colon y\to z$, the
simplicial set $\operatorname{Comp}_X(f,g)$ is connected, i.e., that
$\pi_0^\Delta(\operatorname{Comp}_X(f,g))$ consists of only one element.

\begin{proof}
By the defining pullback on p.~30 and Lemma~1.1.32, vertices
of $K=\operatorname{Comp}_X(f,g)$ are triangles extending the
spine map $(f,g)\colon I^2\to X$. An edge from $\sigma$ to $\tau$
is a map $\Theta\colon\Delta^1\times\Delta^2\to X$ whose ends
are $\sigma,\tau$ and whose restriction to $\Delta^1\times I^2$
is $(f,g)\operatorname{pr}_2$.
The composer property makes $K$ non-empty. We construct such
an edge for any two vertices $\sigma,\tau$.

Put $P=\Delta^1\times\Delta^2=N([1]\times[2])$ and
\[
 A=(\partial\Delta^1\times\Delta^2)\cup(\Delta^1\times I^2).
\]
The maps $\sigma,\tau$ on the two ends and
$(f,g)\operatorname{pr}_2$ on the remaining piece agree on
their intersections, since both triangles restrict to $(f,g)$
on the spine. Thus they define a map $A\to X$.
Label the vertices of $P$ by
\[
 a=(0,0),\ b=(0,1),\ c=(0,2),\quad
 d=(1,0),\ e=(1,1),\ u=(1,2),
\]
and write $[v_0\cdots v_r]$ for the simplex on the indicated
ordered chain. Extend the map successively as follows:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
  \text{simplex added}&\text{known faces}&\text{missing face}&\text{horn}\\ \hline
  [abu]&[ab],\,[bu]&[au]&\Lambda_1^2\\[0pt]
  [abeu]&[abe],\,[abu],\,[beu]&[aeu]&\Lambda_1^3\\[0pt]
  [abcu]&[abc],\,[abu],\,[bcu]&[acu]&\Lambda_1^3\\[0pt]
  [adeu]&[ade],\,[aeu],\,[deu]&[adu]&\Lambda_2^3
 \end{array}
\]
In each row, the intersection with the preceding union is
exactly the union of the listed known faces, as is checked
from the vertex chains. Hence the prescribed faces are
compatible. The first extension uses
$I^2=\Lambda_1^2\hookrightarrow\Delta^2$ and the composer
property; the other three use the assumed inner $3$-horn
extension property. Each filler therefore glues to the
previous map.

The three tetrahedra in the table are exactly the maximal
chains of $[1]\times[2]$, so their union is $P$.
We have obtained the required $\Theta$, hence an edge of $K$
from $\sigma$ to $\tau$. Since $K$ is non-empty and every pair
of vertices is joined, Definition~1.1.9 gives
$\pi_0^\Delta(K)=\{*\}$.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
