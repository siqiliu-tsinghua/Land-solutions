\begin{exercise}{87}
Suppose that $f\colon\mathcal C\to\mathcal D$ is a Joyal equivalence. Then show
that the restricted map $\mathcal C^{\simeq}\to\mathcal D^{\simeq}$ is also a
Joyal equivalence.

\begin{proof}
Functors preserve equivalences, so Lemma~1.2.27 gives the restriction
$f^{\simeq}\colon\mathcal C^{\simeq}\to\mathcal D^{\simeq}$.
By Proposition~2.3.5, $f$ is fully faithful and essentially surjective;
Lemma~2.3.9 gives the same properties for $f^{\simeq}$.
Its source and target are $\infty$-categories by Corollary~1.2.28,
so Theorem~2.3.20 shows that $f^{\simeq}$ is a Joyal equivalence.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
