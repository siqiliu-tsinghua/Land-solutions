\begin{exercise}{47}
Let $\mathcal C_0\subseteq\mathcal C$ be a full sub-$\infty$-category and let $\mathcal D$ be an $\infty$-category. Show that
the functor category $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal D,\mathcal C_0)$ is the full sub-$\infty$-category of $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal D,\mathcal C)$ on those functors
$f\colon\mathcal D\to\mathcal C$ which factor through the inclusion $\mathcal C_0\subseteq\mathcal C$.

\begin{proof}
Put $A=(\mathcal C_0)_0$.
By fullness and Definition~1.2.76, a simplex of $\mathcal C$
belongs to $\mathcal C_0$ exactly when all its vertices lie in $A$.
Consequently a map $Z\to\mathcal C$ factors through
$\mathcal C_0$ exactly when it sends $Z_0$ into $A$;
the factorization is unique because the inclusion is a monomorphism.

Both functor simplicial sets are $\infty$-categories by
Corollary~1.3.38. By Lemma~1.1.32, an $n$-simplex of
$\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal D,\mathcal C)$ is a map
$F\colon\Delta^n\times\mathcal D\to\mathcal C$.
Its vertex functors are $F_i=F|_{\{i\}\times\mathcal D}$.
The preceding criterion shows that $F$ factors through
$\mathcal C_0$ if and only if $F(i,d)\in A$ for every
$i\in[n]$ and $d\in\mathcal D_0$, equivalently if and only
if every $F_i$ factors through $\mathcal C_0$.
Postcomposition with the inclusion is injective in every degree.
Its image therefore consists precisely of the simplices
whose vertex functors factor through $\mathcal C_0$.
By Definition~1.2.76 this is the required full sub-$\infty$-category.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
