\begin{exercise}{156}
Let $f\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}$ and $f'\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E}$ be composable functors which admit right
adjoints $g$ and $g'$, respectively. Show that in this case $gg'$ is a right adjoint of $f'f$.

\begin{proof}
Let $\varepsilon\colon fg\to\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{D}}$ and
$\varepsilon'\colon f'g'\to\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{E}}$ be the counits.
Their whiskered composite is a natural transformation
\[
 \theta\colon f'fgg'
 \xrightarrow{\ f'\varepsilon g'\ }f'g'
 \xrightarrow{\ \varepsilon'\ }\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{E}}.
\]
For $c\in\mathcal{C}$ and $e\in\mathcal{E}$, applying $f'f$ and
postcomposing with $\theta_e$ is the composite
\[
 \mathrm{map}_{\mathcal{C}}(c,gg'e)
 \xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ }\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal{D}}(fc,g'e)
 \xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ }\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal{E}}(f'fc,e).
\]
The first arrow applies $f$ and postcomposes with
$\varepsilon_{g'e}$; the second applies $f'$ and postcomposes with
$\varepsilon'_e$.
Both are the adjunction equivalences of Proposition~5.1.5.
Thus $\theta$ satisfies the mapping-space criterion of
Proposition~5.1.13, which proves $f'f\dashv gg'$.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
