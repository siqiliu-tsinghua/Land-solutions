\begin{exercise}{2}
Work out at least three of the following \emph{simplicial identities}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt,parsep=0pt,
                  topsep=.5\baselineskip]
 \item $d_i^*d_j^*=d_{j-1}^*d_i^*$ if $i<j$
 \item $d_i^*s_j^*=s_{j-1}^*d_i^*$ if $i<j$
 \item $d_i^*s_j^*=\mathrm{id}$ if $i=j,j+1$
 \item $d_i^*s_j^*=s_j^*d_{i-1}^*$ if $i>j+1$\footnote{The original
 condition is $i>i+1$, which is impossible. The intended condition is
 $i>j+1$: in this range the maps in $\Delta$ satisfy
 $s_jd_i=d_{i-1}s_j$, whose image under $X$ is the displayed identity.}
 \item $s_i^*s_j^*=s_{j+1}^*s_i^*$ if $i\le j$
\end{enumerate}
Here, for any simplicial set $X\colon\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Set}$,
we denote the map $X(d_i)$ by $d_i^*$. Hint: Think about what this
means for the maps $d_i$ and $s_j$ in $\Delta$, and prove the
corresponding identities there.

\begin{proof}
Work first in $\Delta$, with the maps following Definition~1.1.5
(pp.~5--6). All composites below are read from right to left, at the
levels where they are defined. We claim
\begin{align*}
 d_jd_i&=d_id_{j-1} &&(i<j),\\
 s_js_i&=s_is_{j+1} &&(i\le j),\\
 s_jd_i&=
 \begin{cases}
   d_is_{j-1},&i<j,\\
   \mathrm{id},&i=j\text{ or }i=j+1,\\
   d_{i-1}s_j,&i>j+1.
 \end{cases}
\end{align*}
The first equality compares two increasing injections omitting exactly
$i$ and $j$. In the second, both surjections identify precisely the
adjacent pairs at positions $(i,i+1)$ and $(j+1,j+2)$.
For the mixed composite, $d_i$ skips $i$ and $s_j$ identifies $j,j+1$:
skipping either of these two values gives the identity; skipping a value
before them leaves the omission at $i$ and the repeated position at
$j-1$; skipping a value after them leaves the omission at $i-1$ and the
repeated position at $j$. These are exactly the three displayed cases.

Now apply $X\colon\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Set}$.
Contravariance reverses every composite, giving respectively (1), (5),
and (2)--(4).
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
