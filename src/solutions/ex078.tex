\begin{exercise}{78}
Show that a functor $p\colon\mathcal C\to\mathcal D$ is conservative if and only if the
following diagram is a pullback:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
 \mathcal C^{\simeq} \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & \mathcal C \arrow[d] \\
 \mathcal D^{\simeq} \arrow[r] & \mathcal D
\end{tikzcd}
\]

\begin{proof}
Functors preserve equivalences, since their homotopy functors
preserve isomorphisms (Definition~1.2.22).
By Lemma~1.2.27, a simplex belongs to the maximal
sub-$\infty$-groupoid exactly when all its edges are equivalences.
Thus $p$ restricts to a map
$\mathcal C^{\simeq}\to\mathcal D^{\simeq}$, making the square
commute.

Since limits of simplicial sets are computed degreewise,
the pullback $P=\mathcal C\times_{\mathcal D}\mathcal D^{\simeq}$
is the simplicial subset of $\mathcal C$ consisting of simplices
whose edges become equivalences under $p$.
The canonical map $\mathcal C^{\simeq}\to P$ is an inclusion.
If $p$ is conservative, each of those edges is already an
equivalence in $\mathcal C$, so Lemma~1.2.27 gives
$P=\mathcal C^{\simeq}$.
Conversely, this equality in degree $1$ says that every edge
whose image is an equivalence is itself an equivalence.
This is precisely conservativity.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
