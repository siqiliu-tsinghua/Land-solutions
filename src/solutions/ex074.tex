\begin{exercise}{74}
Show that an inner fibration $f\colon X\to Y$ is inner-anodyne if and only if it is an
isomorphism.

\begin{proof}
If $f$ is inner-anodyne, it has the LLP with respect
to every inner fibration, hence to itself
(Definition~1.3.2).
The commutative square
\[
 \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
  X \arrow[r,equal] \arrow[d,"f"'] & X \arrow[d,"f"] \\
  Y \arrow[r,equal] \arrow[ur,dashed,"g"] & Y
 \end{tikzcd}
\]
therefore has a diagonal $g$ satisfying
$gf=\mathrm{id}_X$ and $fg=\mathrm{id}_Y$, so $f$ is
an isomorphism.
Conversely, an isomorphism has the LLP with respect
to every map: compose the top map of any lifting
square with its inverse.
Thus it is inner-anodyne by the same definition.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
