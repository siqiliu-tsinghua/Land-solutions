\begin{exercise}{124}
Let $X\to Y$ be a cocartesian fibration and $f\colon K\to Y^{\sharp}$ a map of marked
simplicial sets. Show that $\mathrm{Map}^{\flat}_f(K,X^{\natural})$ is an $\infty$-category and that $\mathrm{Map}^{\sharp}_f(K,X^{\natural})$ is the
largest sub-$\infty$-groupoid inside $\mathrm{Map}^{\flat}_f(K,X^{\natural})$.

\begin{proof}
By Remark~3.2.15,
\[
 A=\mathrm{Map}^{\flat}_f(K,X^{\natural})
   =\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{mcc}}_f(K,X),
\]
which is an $\infty$-category by the proof of Exercise~\ref{ex:122}.
An $n$-simplex of $\mathrm{Map}^{\sharp}_f(K,X^{\natural})$ is exactly
an $n$-simplex of $A$ whose restriction along every edge
$\Delta^1\to\Delta^n$ satisfies the marked-map condition in that exercise.
Indeed, the marked edges of $K\times(\Delta^n)^{\sharp}$ are precisely
the pairs of a marked edge of $K$ and any edge of $\Delta^n$;
each such pair is tested in the corresponding restriction.
Exercise~\ref{ex:122} identifies this condition with every edge of the
$A$-simplex being an equivalence.
Lemma~1.2.27 therefore gives
\[
 \mathrm{Map}^{\sharp}_f(K,X^{\natural})=A^{\simeq}.
\]
By Corollary~1.2.28, this is the largest sub-$\infty$-groupoid of $A$.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
