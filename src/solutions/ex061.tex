\begin{exercise}{61}
Show that if a morphism $f\colon\Delta^1\to\mathcal C$ in an $\infty$-category extends over the
inclusion $\Delta^1\to J$, then $f$ is an equivalence.

\begin{proof}
Let $F\colon J\to\mathcal C$ extend $f$, so $f=F(e)$,
where $e\colon0\to1$ is the distinguished edge of $J$.
Put $g=F(e^{-1})$.
The two simplices $(0,1,0)$ and $(1,0,1)$ of $J$
have successive edges $e,e^{-1}$ and $e^{-1},e$,
respectively, with long edges $\mathrm{id}_0$ and $\mathrm{id}_1$.
Their images under $F$ therefore give
\[
 [g][f]=\mathrm{id}_{F(0)},\qquad
 [f][g]=\mathrm{id}_{F(1)}
 \quad\text{in }h\mathcal C
\]
by Definition~1.2.5.
Thus $[f]$ is an isomorphism, and $f$ is an equivalence
by Definition~1.2.22.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
