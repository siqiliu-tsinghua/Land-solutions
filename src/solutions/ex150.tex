\begin{exercise}{150}
Give an example of a proper (or smooth) map which is not universally proper
(or smooth).

\begin{proof}
Choose a connected acyclic Kan complex $K$ with
$\pi_1(|K|)\ne1$.
For an explicit choice, take the singular complex of the presentation
complex $W$ with two generators $x,y$ and relators
$x^3y^{-5}$, $x^3(xy)^{-2}$.
Its cellular differential in degrees $2,1$ is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&1\\-5&-2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
of determinant $-1$, so $W$ is acyclic.
The assignments $x\mapsto(1\,2\,3)$ and
$y\mapsto(1\,4\,3\,2\,5)$ satisfy the relations in $A_5$
and give a nontrivial fundamental group.
Since $|K|\simeq W$, the suspension $\Sigma|K|$ is simply connected
and acyclic, hence contractible by Hurewicz and Whitehead.
Consequently
\[
 |T|*|K|\simeq\Sigma(|T|\wedge|K|)\simeq *
 \qquad(T\ne\varnothing),
\]
where $*$ denotes the topological join and basepoints are chosen at
vertices.

Set
\[
 X=(\{0\}\amalg\{1\})\star\{2\},\qquad
 Y=(K\amalg\{1\})\star\{2\},
\]
and let $p\colon Y\to X$ collapse $K$ to $0$ and fix $1,2$.
We verify properness using Remark~4.4.21.
Let $i\colon A\to X$ be coinitial and put
$A_r=A\times_X\{r\}$.
Since $X_{/0}=\{0\}$, $X_{/1}=\{1\}$, and $X_{/2}\cong X$,
the simplicial sets $A_0,A_1,A$ are weakly contractible.
Write $B=A\times_XY$ and $D=A\times_X\Delta^{\{1,2\}}$;
in particular $D$ is nonempty since it contains $A_1$.

We first show that $B$ is weakly contractible.
There is a comparison over $A$
\[
 Q=(A\times K)\amalg_{D\times K}D\longrightarrow B:
\]
on a simplex mapping to a block of $0$'s followed by $2$'s, retain
the $K$-coordinate on the $0$-block and append the cone vertices;
on $D$ discard the $K$-coordinate.
These prescriptions commute with faces and degeneracies.
Over a simplex $\sigma\colon\Delta^n\to A$ mapping constantly to $0$,
the comparison is the identity on $\Delta^n\times K$;
over one mapping into $\Delta^{\{1,2\}}$, it is the identity on $\Delta^n$.
Otherwise the $0$-block is $\{0,\ldots,m\}$, with $m<n$.
Putting $F=\Delta^{\{m+1,\ldots,n\}}$, the two pullbacks are
\[
 Q_\sigma=(\Delta^n\times K)\amalg_{F\times K}F,
 \qquad B_\sigma=(\Delta^m\times K)\star F.
\]
Both are weakly contractible: the latter is a join with the nonempty
simplex $F$, and the homotopy from $\mathrm{id}_{[n]}$ to
$r(t)=\max(t,m+1)$ retracts the former onto $F$.
Thus $Q\to B$ is a weak equivalence by skeletal gluing over $A$:
pullback preserves the simplex-attachment pushouts, their boundary
maps remain monomorphisms, and weak equivalences are preserved by
gluing and unions along monomorphisms.

Since $D\times K\to A\times K$ is a monomorphism, realization of
the defining pushout of $Q$ is a homotopy pushout.
Using $|A|\simeq *$ and realization's preservation of products gives
\[
 |Q|\simeq
 \operatorname{hpo}\bigl(|K|\leftarrow|D|\times|K|\to|D|\bigr)
 \simeq |D|*|K|\simeq *.
\]
Hence $B$ is weakly contractible.
For $j\colon B\to Y$, its relative slices are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B\times_Y Y_{/k}&\cong A_0\times K_{/k} &&(k\in K),\\
 B\times_Y Y_{/1}&\cong A_1,\qquad
 B\times_Y Y_{/2}\cong B.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here $K_{/k}$ has terminal object $\mathrm{id}_k$ and is weakly
contractible. All these slices are therefore weakly contractible,
so $j$ is coinitial. This proves that $p$ is proper.

Its base change to $S=\Delta^{\{0,2\}}\subset X$ is
$K\star\{2\}\to S$.
The coinitial inclusion $\{0\}\to S$ pulls back to
$K\to K\star\{2\}$, whose relative slice at $2$ is $K$.
This is not weakly contractible, so that base change is not proper.
Thus $p$ is not universally proper.
Taking opposites and applying Exercise~\ref{ex:145}, together with
the compatibility of opposites with pullbacks, gives a smooth map
$p^{\mathrm{op}}$ which is not universally smooth.
\end{proof}
\end{exercise}
